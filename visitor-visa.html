What is a Visitor Visa?

A Temporary Resident Visa (TRV) lets you enter Canada for a temporary stay.

A Canadian visitor visa, officially a Temporary Resident Visa (TRV), is the document that allows a foreign national to travel to and enter Canada for a temporary purpose — tourism, visiting family, or business. It is issued by IRCC and, for most applicants, is required before boarding a flight to Canada. Citizens of visa-exempt countries typically need an eTA instead of a TRV.

Whether you are applying from outside Canada for the first time, or you are already here and need to extend your visitor status, the core question an officer asks is the same: are your ties to your home country strong enough that you will leave Canada at the end of your authorized stay?

Who Applies for a Visitor Visa

  • First-time visitors from abroad — tourism, visiting family or friends, or short business trips.
  • People already in Canada extending their stay — see visitor visa processing from inside Canada.
  • Parents and grandparents who want longer stays may qualify for a Super Visa instead.
  • Applicants who were previously refused and need a stronger, better-documented application.
