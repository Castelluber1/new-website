Francophone Mobility Program Canada 2026: Work Permit, Requirements & PR

10 min read•2026-09-18

The Francophone Mobility Program is one of the most useful and least understood ways to work in Canada. If you speak French, it can let a Canadian employer hire you without going through a Labour Market Impact Assessment, which is the slow and expensive step that blocks many work permit applications. This guide explains what the Francophone Mobility Program is, who qualifies, how the work permit works, and how it can lead to permanent residence in 2026.

Is the Francophone Mobility Program ending or closing?

This is the first question most applicants ask, because policy around temporary programs has been changing. As of September 2026, the Francophone Mobility work permit is open and IRCC's official program pages are active. Because immigration rules change, confirm the current status on IRCC's website before you build your plan. The rest of this guide explains how it works and how to use it before anything changes.

What is the Francophone Mobility Program?

The Francophone Mobility Program lets Canadian employers outside Quebec hire French-speaking foreign workers without a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA). It runs under the International Mobility Program, and the work permit it issues carries the exemption code C16.

Because it is LMIA-exempt, the employer does not have to prove they could not find a Canadian for the role. That removes weeks of processing and a large government fee, which is why French speakers often reach a Canadian work permit faster than other applicants.

Who runs the Francophone Mobility Program

The program is administered by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) as part of the International Mobility Program. It supports Canada's goal of growing French-speaking communities outside Quebec, so the job must be located in a province or territory other than Quebec.

Francophone Mobility Program requirements

To qualify for the Francophone Mobility Program, you generally need to meet three requirements.

Language requirement

You must prove that your French speaking and listening skills are at an intermediate level, equivalent to NCLC 5 or higher on the Niveaux de compétence linguistique canadiens scale. Reading and writing are not assessed for this work permit. The clearest proof is a result from an approved test such as TEF Canada or TCF Canada, and the officer must be satisfied by the evidence you provide.

Job offer and employer requirements

You need a genuine offer of employment from a Canadian employer for a position outside Quebec. The job can be in any TEER category of the National Occupational Classification (NOC), except primary agriculture occupations in TEER 4 and 5. Before you apply, your employer must submit the offer through IRCC's Employer Portal under exemption code C16 (Mobilité Francophone), pay the $230 employer compliance fee, and give you the 7-digit offer of employment number you will need for your application.

Eligible location

The job must be located outside Quebec. Quebec runs its own immigration streams, so Francophone Mobility applies to the rest of Canada, from British Columbia to the Atlantic provinces.

Francophone Mobility Program work permit

The Francophone Mobility Program issues an employer-specific work permit under exemption code C16. It ties you to the employer named on the offer, and its length usually matches the job offer, up to the maximum the officer approves.

How the C16 work permit works

Once your employer submits the offer of employment and pays the compliance fee, you apply for the work permit and reference the offer. Because there is no LMIA, the file skips the entire labour market review step. You still go through the normal work permit checks: admissibility, medical if required, and biometrics.

Processing time

Processing times depend on where you apply from and on current IRCC volumes. Check the current estimate for your situation on IRCC's official processing times tool before planning your arrival date.

Work permit extension

You can apply to extend a Francophone Mobility work permit if your job continues and you still meet the requirements. Apply before your current permit expires so you keep maintained status and can keep working while the extension is processed.

Francophone Mobility Program benefits

The Francophone Mobility Program has advantages that a standard work permit does not.

  • No LMIA. The employer skips the Labour Market Impact Assessment, which saves the government fee and weeks of processing.
  • Open to more jobs. Occupations in every TEER category qualify, except primary agriculture occupations in TEER 4 and 5.
  • Employer-friendly. Because there is no recruitment test to pass, employers are often more willing to hire through this route.
  • A bridge to permanent residence. Canadian work experience gained on this permit can support a future Express Entry application. Note that Express Entry's French-language category requires NCLC 7 in all four abilities, a higher level than this work permit requires.

Francophone Mobility Program timelines

A realistic timeline runs in three stages: securing a qualifying job offer, getting the work permit, and later moving toward permanent residence.

  1. Job offer and Employer Portal submission. Your employer submits the offer of employment through the Employer Portal and pays the compliance fee before you apply.
  2. Work permit application and processing. You apply and wait for the processing time above.
  3. Time in Canada, then permanent residence. Canadian work experience plus your French ability set you up for an Express Entry application.

Plan for several months from offer to arrival, and longer for the full path to permanent residence. Starting your French test early is the single best way to avoid delay.

From Francophone Mobility to permanent residence

The Francophone Mobility work permit is temporary, but it is one of the strongest bridges to permanent residence Canada offers to French speakers.

Once you have Canadian work experience, it can strengthen your Express Entry profile. French ability can also add points in the Comprehensive Ranking System, and IRCC runs category-based rounds for French-speaking candidates. These rounds have often invited candidates at scores below those of general rounds. There is an important difference, though. Francophone Mobility requires NCLC 5 in speaking and listening, while the Express Entry French-language category requires NCLC 7 in all four abilities (reading, writing, listening and speaking). Many applicants plan to retest once in Canada to reach that level.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Francophone Mobility Program open or closed in 2026?

It is open as of September 2026 and continues under the International Mobility Program. Confirm the current status on IRCC's official pages before applying.

What French level do you need for Francophone Mobility?

You need an intermediate level in French speaking and listening, equivalent to NCLC 5 or higher. The clearest proof is an approved test such as TEF Canada or TCF Canada. IRCC's eligibility criteria for this permit do not include an English requirement.

Does the Francophone Mobility Program lead to PR?

Not directly. It is a temporary, employer-specific work permit. The Canadian work experience it provides can support a future Express Entry application. To qualify for Express Entry's French-language category, you need NCLC 7 in all four abilities, which is higher than the NCLC 5 in speaking and listening this permit requires.

Do you need a job offer for Francophone Mobility?

Yes. You need a genuine offer of employment from a Canadian employer for a position outside Quebec, in any TEER category except primary agriculture occupations in TEER 4 and 5. The employer must submit the offer through the Employer Portal and pay the $230 compliance fee before you apply.

Can the employer be anywhere in Canada?

The employer and job must be located outside Quebec. Quebec manages its own immigration programs, so the Francophone Mobility Program covers every other province and territory.
