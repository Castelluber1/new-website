Working in Canada Without an LMIA: Exempt Categories

6 min read•Oct 02, 2025

Not every work permit in Canada requires a Labour Market Impact Assessment. Several categories let a foreign worker be hired without one, usually because the role carries a recognized public, cultural, or economic benefit for Canada.

What does "LMIA-exempt" mean?

Normally, a Canadian employer must obtain a positive LMIA before hiring a foreign worker, to show that no Canadian was available for the role. Certain work permits skip that step. These fall under the International Mobility Program (IMP), which covers situations where hiring a foreign national is considered to benefit Canada directly. Skipping the LMIA often means lower cost and a simpler process for the employer.

Common exempt categories

  • Free-trade agreements (CUSMA/USMCA and others): professionals and business people covered by trade agreements between Canada and their country.
  • Intra-company transfers (ICT): employees moved from a foreign branch of a company to its Canadian office.
  • Researchers, academics, and artists: roles tied to universities, cultural work, or reciprocal exchange programs.
  • Spouses and certain family members of some permit holders, depending on the situation.

Things to keep in mind

Each exempt category has its own conditions, and being "LMIA-exempt" is not the same as being "work-permit-exempt" — in most cases you still need a work permit, just without the LMIA step. Because the categories are specific and the eligibility evidence matters, it's worth confirming which one (if any) applies to your situation with a licensed consultant before you apply.
