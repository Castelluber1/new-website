Permanent Residence

TR to PR in Canada: Turning Your Work or Study Permit into Permanent Residence (2026)

10 min read•2026-09-29

If you are already in Canada on a work permit, a study permit, or a post-graduation work permit (PGWP), you may have advantages over someone applying from outside the country: your Canadian time, work, and study are worth real points and open routes that overseas applicants cannot use. This guide explains how the temporary-to-permanent (TR to PR) transition actually works, which routes fit which situations, and what to line up now so you are ready when the window opens.

Why being in Canada already puts you ahead

Permanent residence is the status that lets you live and work anywhere in Canada indefinitely, without a permit tied to one employer or school. Most people who get there through the economic system do it through one of two routes: Express Entry and the Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs). Being physically in Canada on a temporary status helps with both, for concrete reasons:

  • Canadian work experience counts. Skilled work experience gained in Canada is worth points in Express Entry and is the core of the Canadian Experience Class.
  • PGWP holders can use the open work permit after graduation to gain skilled work experience (TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3), which is what the Canadian Experience Class requires.

The policy direction backs this up. Canada's 2026-2028 Immigration Levels Plan stabilizes permanent-resident admissions at about 380,000 per year, with economic immigration making up roughly 64% of admissions by 2027-2028. At the same time, the government is reducing new temporary arrivals and creating targeted pathways for people already here, including a one-time initiative to transition up to 33,000 skilled temporary workers in specific in-demand sectors, with a focus on rural areas, to permanent residence in 2026 and 2027. In our reading, the direction is clear: the plan gives more weight to people who are already in Canada, working and settled.

Route 1: Express Entry (including the Canadian Experience Class)

Express Entry is the online system that manages applications for three federal economic programs, ranks candidates with a score (the Comprehensive Ranking System, or CRS), and invites the top candidates in periodic draws.

For someone already in Canada, the most relevant program inside Express Entry is usually the Canadian Experience Class (CEC), built specifically for people with Canadian skilled work experience. To qualify, you generally need at least one year of skilled work experience in Canada (TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3) within the three years before you apply. Self-employment and work done while you were a full-time student do not count.

Category-based selection in 2026. On top of general draws, IRCC runs draws that target specific categories. For 2026, the priority categories are:

  • New for 2026: physicians with Canadian work experience, researchers, senior managers (across four industry groupings), transport occupations, and skilled military recruits (recruited by the Canadian Armed Forces).
  • Renewed: French-language proficiency, healthcare and social services, education, STEM, and the trades.
  • Removed: the agriculture and agri-food category (the only one dropped for 2026).

One change worth knowing: for the renewed categories, IRCC raised the minimum work-experience requirement to one year, up from six months. The physicians, researchers and senior managers categories require at least 12 months of work experience in Canada. The transport category accepts experience gained in Canada or abroad within the last three years. In practice this means the timeline to qualify for a category draw is longer than it used to be, so plan your experience accordingly.

Route 2: Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs)

Every province and territory (except Quebec, which runs its own system) has its own immigration streams tuned to its labour market. Many PNP streams favour candidates who are already working in the province or who have a job offer there. If your permit ties you to an employer in a specific province, that province's PNP is often the most direct path worth investigating first. If you receive a nomination through an Express Entry-aligned provincial stream, you get 600 additional CRS points, which typically leads to an invitation in a following draw.

Route 3: Bridging while you wait

A practical worry for people mid-transition: what happens to your right to work if your permit expires while your PR application is in process? Depending on your situation, a Bridging Open Work Permit (BOWP) may let you keep working while you wait for a decision. Until December 31, 2026, a temporary measure allows provincial nominee applicants in Canada to apply for a bridging open work permit before receiving their acknowledgement of receipt (AOR), using proof that their PR application was submitted. (See our guide on the bridging open work permit for the details.)

What to line up now, before you apply

You do not need everything perfect to start, but the strongest TR to PR files tend to have these in order early:

  • Language test results that reflect your real ability, redone if your scores are old or if your English or French has improved.
  • A clear record of your Canadian work: pay stubs, employment letters stating your job title, duties, and hours, and the correct occupation classification.
  • Your educational credentials assessed, if you studied outside Canada and plan to use that education for points.
  • A realistic read of your ranking against recent draw cut-offs, so you know whether to apply now or raise your score first.

This is exactly the kind of situation where a review with a regulated consultant can help you identify gaps before you apply, since the difference between applying now and waiting can come down to a single document or a few points.

FAQ

Can I apply for PR while on a work permit? Yes. Being on a valid work permit does not stop you from applying for permanent residence, and Canadian work experience generally strengthens your application.

Does my PGWP count toward PR? Work on a PGWP can count toward the Canadian Experience Class if it is skilled work (TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3). Work in TEER 4 or 5 occupations and self-employment do not count for CEC.

Do I have to leave Canada to become a permanent resident? No. Many people complete the entire transition from inside Canada.

What happens to my work authorization while my PR application is processing? It depends on your permit and timing. A bridging open work permit may allow you to keep working while your PR application is processed. Until December 31, 2026, provincial nominee applicants can apply before receiving their AOR.

How long does the TR to PR transition take? It varies widely by route, program, and your file. Check current processing times on the IRCC website.

Final thoughts

If you are in Canada on a permit, the smartest move is not to wait until it is expiring to think about permanent residence. Your Canadian time is an asset that compounds: more experience, better language, a stronger profile. The routes are real, but they reward preparation and timing, and the 2026 rules reward it a little more patiently than before.
