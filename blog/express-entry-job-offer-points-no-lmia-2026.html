Express Entry Job Offer Points: The New Rules (No LMIA, as of November 2026)

10 min read•2026-11-03

[lead]

On November 3, 2026, IRCC changed how a job offer earns Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points in Express Entry, and removed the Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) requirement for those points. This is the most meaningful change to CRS scoring in years for anyone with a Canadian job offer. But the points are not a flat bonus, and they are not for every job: they depend on the wage of the offer, they apply only to high-wage occupations, and they do not stack with your Canadian work experience points. Below is exactly what changed, who gains, how much, and what to do about it, with every figure drawn from the official IRCC announcement.

What changed on November 3, 2026

Two things changed at once, and they work together:

  1. A qualifying job offer now earns CRS points again, on a wage-based scale, instead of the old flat structure.
  2. An LMIA is no longer required for that job offer to earn the new points.

For years, the job offer points in Express Entry were tied to having an LMIA, which is a separate, slow, employer-driven process. That requirement is what made job-offer points out of reach for most candidates. As of November 3, 2026, IRCC removed it for qualifying job offers. A temporary public policy also exempts candidates invited on or after that date from LMIA and LMIA-exemption requirements for qualifying job offers used under the Federal Skilled Worker Program and the Federal Skilled Trades Program, provided they meet the policy's conditions.

This does not mean "any job offer, no checks." IRCC still verifies that the offer is genuine (more on that below). It means the LMIA document is no longer the gatekeeper.

How many points a job offer is worth now

The new job-offer points apply only to high-wage occupations, and the amount depends on how the offered wage compares to the median wage for that occupation:

Offered wage vs. median CRS points
At least 2 times the median wage 100 points
At least 1.5 times the median wage 70 points
At least 1.3 times the median wage 50 points

So the headline "100 points" is real, but it is the top of a scale, and it requires an offer paying at least double the median wage for that occupation. A strong but more typical high-wage offer lands at 70 or 50 points. An offer below 1.3 times the median does not earn these points.

The catch most summaries leave out: it does not stack

This is the part that trips people up. Express Entry also awards points for Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation, on its own scale (each tier requires at least 12 months of such work):

  • 70 points for work at 2 times the median wage
  • 40 points for combined work at 1.5 times the median or above (if not eligible for 70)
  • 20 points for combined work in eligible high-wage occupations at 1.3 times the median or above (if not eligible for 40 or 70)

These experience points are added to the base points you already earn for how long you have worked (up to 80 points for five years), and the combined maximum for Canadian high-wage experience is 150 points.

Here is the rule that matters: the job-offer points and the high-wage experience points do not stack. If you qualify under both, you receive whichever one gives you more, not the sum. IRCC also caps the whole labour-market-integration factor at 150 points without an accompanying spouse or common-law partner, and 140 points with one.

In plain terms: do not add 100 job-offer points on top of 70 experience points and expect 170. You get the larger of the two, within the cap.

Who actually gains from this

The candidates who benefit most are:

  • People with a high-wage Canadian job offer but no LMIA. This is the big one. The offer can now earn points on its own.
  • Candidates whose CRS score has been sitting just below recent cut-offs. Even 50 points can change which draws you are competitive in.
  • Workers in genuinely high-wage roles where the offer realistically reaches 1.3x the median or more.

The candidates who gain little or nothing:

  • Anyone whose offer is below 1.3 times the median wage for the occupation.
  • Candidates who already max out the high-wage experience points, since the offer points do not add on top.
  • Low-wage roles, which these points do not cover.

What counts as a "qualifying" job offer

The LMIA is gone, but the offer still has to clear a bar. Under the new rules, a qualifying job offer generally must be:

  • Full-time and continuous, for work in Canada of at least one year after you become a permanent resident. (Specific requirements apply to skilled trades and to certain physicians.)
  • Genuine, in the officer's judgment. An IRCC officer assesses whether the employer is actively engaged in the business, whether the offer reflects a reasonable employment need, whether the employer can reasonably meet the terms, whether you are qualified and likely to accept and do the work, and whether the employer complies with applicable employment and recruitment laws.
  • Still valid. A revoked offer no longer qualifies.

And the Canadian work experience that feeds the experience-points side must generally have been earned in Canada within the previous 10 years, in an occupation at NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3. (Specific provisions apply to certain physicians providing publicly funded medical services.)

What to do before and after the change

IRCC's own guidance is direct: candidates who may qualify for job-offer points should review and update their Express Entry profile so that all required job-offer information is included and accurate. If your profile does not reflect the offer correctly, the system cannot award the points.

One more honest caveat, straight from IRCC: additional CRS points do not guarantee an invitation. Invitations still depend on each round's requirements and the CRS cut-off score for that draw. More points improve your position; they do not remove the draw from the equation.

If you are not sure whether your offer reaches the wage thresholds, whether it counts as high-wage, or whether the offer or your experience gives you more points, that is exactly the kind of question worth getting right before you change your profile. A wrong entry can cost you points or flag a profile.

Two related pieces often decide more of your score than the job offer itself. Your Express Entry language requirements set a large share of your CRS, and candidates who already have Canadian work history should check whether the Canadian Experience Class is the stronger route for them. If a federal draw stays out of reach, the Provincial Nominee Programs remain the single biggest CRS lever, worth 600 points on their own.

Frequently asked questions

Do I still need an LMIA to get job-offer points in Express Entry? No. As of November 3, 2026, an LMIA is not required for a qualifying job offer to earn the new CRS points. IRCC still verifies that the offer is genuine.

How many points does a job offer give now? It depends on the wage: 100 points for an offer at least 2 times the median wage for the occupation, 70 points at 1.5 times, and 50 points at 1.3 times. It applies only to high-wage occupations.

Can I add the job-offer points to my Canadian experience points? No. If you qualify for both, you get whichever is higher, not the sum. The whole labour-market-integration factor is capped at 150 points (140 with a spouse or common-law partner).

Does this guarantee I will be invited? No. Extra points improve your ranking, but invitations still depend on each draw's cut-off score and requirements.

I have an offer but my profile does not show it. What should I do? Update your Express Entry profile so the job-offer details are complete and accurate. If the information is missing or wrong, the points will not be applied.

Where this leaves you

This change reopens a door that had been effectively closed for most candidates: a job offer can lift your CRS score without the LMIA bottleneck. But it rewards high-wage offers specifically, it does not stack with experience points, and it does not override the cut-off in any given draw. The smart move is to confirm which side (offer or experience) actually gives you more, make sure your profile reflects it accurately, and plan your timing around the draws, not just the points.
