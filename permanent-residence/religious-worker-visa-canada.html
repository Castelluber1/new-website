Working in Canada as a religious worker

Who qualifies

Ministers, priests, pastors, imams, monks, nuns, and members of religious orders whose duties are primarily religious, joining a Canadian religious organization whose beliefs they share.

How it works

Many religious workers qualify for an LMIA-exempt work permit under the International Mobility Program, because the work is charitable or religious in nature. We prepare the organization letter, prove eligibility, and file the application.

What you need

  • Invitation from a Canadian religious organization
  • Duties that are primarily religious
  • Proof of your religious training or membership
  • Evidence the organization is genuine and non-profit
  • Admissibility to Canada

Frequently asked questions

Who qualifies as a religious worker in Canada?

A religious worker is someone whose duties are primarily religious, such as a minister, priest, pastor, or member of a religious order, joining a Canadian religious organization whose beliefs they share.

Do religious workers need an LMIA?

Many religious workers qualify for an LMIA-exempt work permit under the International Mobility Program, because the work is charitable or religious in nature. Eligibility depends on the role and the organization.

Can a religious worker become a permanent resident?

Yes. Time spent working in Canada as a religious worker can build the experience and ties that support a permanent residence application through Express Entry or a Provincial Nominee Program.

What documents does a religious worker need?

Typically a letter from the religious organization describing the role, proof the organization is genuine and non-profit, evidence of your religious training or membership, and standard admissibility documents.
