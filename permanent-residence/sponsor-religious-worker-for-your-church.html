Bringing a religious worker to your church

Why it is easier than you think

Religious work is often LMIA-exempt under the International Mobility Program, so your church usually does not need a Labour Market Impact Assessment to invite a worker from abroad.

What your church provides

A letter describing the role and its religious duties, proof your organization is a genuine non-profit religious body, and details of how the worker will be supported. We prepare this with you.

Who your church can bring

  • Pastors, priests, and ministers
  • Imams, monks, and nuns
  • Members of religious orders
  • Workers whose duties are primarily religious
  • People who share your congregation's beliefs

Frequently asked questions

How can a Canadian church bring a religious worker from abroad?

A Canadian church can invite a foreign religious worker through an LMIA-exempt work permit under the International Mobility Program. The church provides a letter describing the role and its religious purpose, and the worker applies for the permit.

Does a church need an LMIA to hire a foreign pastor?

In many cases no. Religious work is often LMIA-exempt under the International Mobility Program because it is charitable or religious in nature, which removes the Labour Market Impact Assessment step for the church.

What documents does the church need to provide?

Typically a letter describing the position and its religious duties, proof the organization is a genuine non-profit religious body, and details of how the worker will be supported while in Canada.

Can the religious worker later become a permanent resident?

Yes. Time working in Canada can build the experience and ties that support a permanent residence application, which helps churches retain the worker long term.
